Privacy Policy

Your clipboard goes only where you decide.

By default CopyC works entirely on your computer. Only if you sign in with Google to turn on sync is text content encrypted on your computer first and then sent to your other devices and your own Google Drive. Files you send with Send files work the same way: they are encrypted and go to your own devices only when you choose to send them. We never sell or share anything, and we can't read what you copy or send.

Policy version 1.6 · Last updated: November 17, 2026 · Applies to all versions of CopyC (Windows, macOS, Microsoft Store and Android versions)

  • Local by default Without sync, what you copy is only used for the confirmation and your history on your own computer. It never leaves it.
  • Sync and file sending are optional and encrypted Sync is off by default, and content is encrypted on your computer, so the relay server can't read it. Images, files and passwords on the clipboard are never synced automatically; files are only sent when you send them, and across networks they wait in your own Google Drive and are deleted after 24 hours.
  • No tracking, no selling No analytics, ads or trackers, and nothing is ever sold or shared.

What CopyC reads

  • Clipboard content: each time you copy, CopyC reads the clipboard to work out what kind of content it is (text, image, files, color, link) and show a confirmation. It listens to the system clipboard event and does not log keystrokes.
  • Your Mac’s model and the camera area of its screen: used only on your Mac, to show the model under About and to let top-center notifications grow out of the Dynamic Island or notch. They are never sent anywhere.
  • The foreground app's name: to show which app you copied from and to apply your app blocklist.
  • Whether an app is fullscreen: to switch to an unobtrusive indicator while you watch videos, play games or present.

What CopyC stores on your computer

  • History: the text, links and color codes you copy, plus image thumbnails and file locations (paths), are saved in a local database on your computer so you can search and copy them again later. By default up to 1,000 entries are kept and the oldest are removed automatically. You can change the limit (100–5,000), delete single entries, clear everything, or turn history off in Preferences → History. Files themselves are never copied or stored.
  • Passwords are not saved by default: content that password managers mark as confidential, and content that looks like a password or key, is not written to history by default. It is only saved if you turn on “Save passwords and sensitive content” (stored in plain text on your computer and still masked in the list). Turning that option off deletes the saved passwords.
  • App blocklist: nothing copied from an app on your blocklist is written to history or synced.
  • “Recent copies” on the Overview: up to 20 items, kept in memory only and gone when you quit the app.
  • Your settings: such as position, language and app blocklist, saved in a settings file on your computer.
  • If you sign in to sync: your Google account email, your Google account ID, a randomly generated device ID, and the authorization token (refresh token) Google issues to CopyC. On Windows the token is kept in Credential Manager; on macOS in a settings file only you can read.

To remove everything, click Clear in Preferences → History (and, if you use sync, Delete cloud data in Preferences → Sync), then uninstall. The Microsoft Store version deletes all of its local data when uninstalled; for the other versions the data lives in “%APPDATA%\top.dreamsworld.copyc” on Windows and “~/Library/Application Support/top.dreamsworld.copyc” on macOS, and you can delete it by hand.

Sync across devices (optional, from 1.1.0)

Sync is off by default and only starts when you click “Sign in with Google” in Preferences → Sync or in the welcome guide. Once on:

  • What is synced: text content only — text, links, email addresses, phone numbers and color codes — together with when it was copied and this computer's name.
  • Never synced automatically: images, files and file paths on the clipboard, passwords and sensitive content (whether or not you save passwords), and anything copied from a blocklisted app. Single entries larger than 256 KB are not synced either.
  • End-to-end encryption: content is encrypted on your computer with XChaCha20-Poly1305. The encryption key is created on the first device you sign in on and stored in CopyC's private folder in your Google Drive, so your other computers on the same Google account can decrypt it. Neither Cloudflare nor our server ever has the key or can read the content.
  • History backup (Google Drive): encrypted entries are stored in the application data folder (appDataFolder) of your own Google Drive. This folder is hidden from your files, and CopyC can only access this folder — never your other files. Backups are kept for 90 days and then deleted by CopyC. Entries you delete on one computer are deleted on the others too.
  • Live sync (Cloudflare): when you copy text, the encrypted content passes through our relay server on Cloudflare (copyc.app.dreamsworld.top) to your other computers that are signed in to the same Google account and online, which put it on their clipboard (you can turn this off). The relay only uses your Google sign-in token to tell which account you belong to, and receives a random ID for each computer (to avoid delivering the same copy twice); it stores no content and does not store your email. As with any network connection, Cloudflare processes connection details such as your IP address.
  • Direct transfer on the same network (from CopyC 1.1.1): with live sync on, CopyC regularly broadcasts an encrypted signal on the network you are connected to (such as your home or office Wi-Fi) to find your other computers on that network that are signed in to the same Google account, then sends the encrypted content to them directly, without going through the internet. Computers that don’t receive it directly still get it through the Cloudflare relay above. The device ID, name and port in the signal are encrypted with your key too, so others on the same network can’t read the content or tell which account it belongs to; they can only see that your computer’s IP address is sending a signal. You can turn this off in Preferences → Sync → “Send directly on the same network”.
  • Google permissions: CopyC only asks for openid, email (to show which account you signed in with) and drive.appdata (read and write CopyC's own hidden folder only).

You can turn off live sync or history backup at any time. Sign out revokes CopyC's access, deletes the token on this computer and removes entries synced from your other computers; Delete cloud data removes all of CopyC's backups and keys from your Google Drive. You can also remove CopyC under third-party access in your Google Account.

CopyC's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements: this data is only used to provide the sync features you can see, is never used for advertising, never sold and never given to third parties, and is not read by humans.

Send files (optional, from desktop 1.2.0 and Android 1.1.0)

You can send files, folders and images to your other computers and phones signed in to the same Google account. Something is only sent when you choose to send it: on a computer by dropping files in, clicking “Choose files”, “Send clipboard” or “Send to…” in the notification; on a phone through the Share menu or “Choose files”. Clipboard content is never sent automatically.

  • On the same network: when the other device is on the same network (found through “Direct transfer on the same network” above), files go directly over your local network — never through the internet, Cloudflare or any server of ours.
  • On different networks: the file is encrypted and kept in the application data folder of your own Google Drive (the same hidden folder as the history backup above), together with an encrypted note of its name, size, hash, sender and recipient. The live sync relay only forwards an encrypted “a file is waiting” notice. The receiving device downloads it only when you tap “Download”, within 24 hours of the upload; after 24 hours any of your devices deletes it automatically, and it shows as expired. Meanwhile it uses your Google Drive storage; you can change the per-file limit in Settings (100 MB by default, up to 200 GB). Uploads that were interrupted are deleted after 48 hours.
  • Device list: so you can see where to send, each device keeps an encrypted entry with its name, platform (Windows, macOS, Android) and supported features in the same application data folder. Signing out removes the list and the received-files list from that device.
  • What is sent: the file content, its name (including the path inside a folder you send), its size, and this device’s name and device ID.
  • End-to-end encryption: every piece is encrypted and authenticated with your account key (the same key as sync above) using XChaCha20-Poly1305, and the whole file is checked with SHA-256 when it arrives. Only devices signed in to the same Google account can decrypt it; others on the network, Google, Cloudflare and we can’t see file names or content.
  • Storage on the receiving device: computers save received files in Downloads/CopyC (changeable in Preferences) and, by default, put them on the clipboard (you can turn this off); phones save them in Download/CopyC, and images in Pictures/CopyC. Partly received files are deleted if not resumed within 7 days. The transfer list is kept in memory only; the list of files received through Google Drive is kept on the device and removed a day after they expire.
  • How to turn it off: nothing is transferred unless you send something. With “Send directly on the same network” off, the device can’t be found on your local network; after signing out it receives no files at all.

Android version

The Android version is a sync companion for CopyC on your computers. It only goes online after you sign in with Google, and uses the same end-to-end encryption, Google Drive app folder and Cloudflare relay as the desktop version (see “Sync across devices” above).

  • Clipboard: text from your computers is put on your phone’s clipboard. Your phone’s clipboard is only read, encrypted and sent when you tap “Send clipboard to computer”, use the Share menu or the Quick Settings tile. CopyC never reads your phone’s clipboard in the background (Android doesn’t allow it).
  • Google sign-in: handled by Google Play services on your phone, with the same permissions as the desktop version: openid, email and drive.appdata. The relay asks Google which account a sign-in token belongs to and does not store the token.
  • History: the phone downloads the encrypted history your computers back up to Google Drive, decrypts it on the phone and keeps it in the app; it is deleted when you sign out. The phone does not upload history of its own yet.
  • Receive in the background (optional): when on, CopyC keeps a persistent notification so it stays connected to your own computers, and tells you with a notification when something arrives (the content is hidden on the lock screen).
  • Send directly on the same network (optional): needs Android’s “Nearby devices” permission and works like the desktop feature described above.
  • Send files: only the files you pick in the Share menu or with “Choose files” are read and sent; no photos or storage permission is needed (see “Send files” above).
  • Nothing else: the Android version has no ads, analytics or trackers, does not read your contacts, location or other apps’ data, and never reads your photos on its own.

Masking and Privacy mode

Content that password managers mark as confidential, and content that looks like a password or key, is masked in confirmations automatically. With Privacy mode on, confirmations, recent copies and the History list show only the content type, never the content, which is handy when sharing your screen.

Update check

The version downloaded from the website asks GitHub for the latest version when it starts (and once a day while it keeps running). From CopyC 1.1.1, when a new version is available it downloads the installer from GitHub in the background and checks our signature with a built-in public key before asking you to click “Restart & update”; a file with a wrong signature is never installed. These requests contain nothing you copied and no personal data; as with any web request, GitHub sees your IP address. You can turn this off in Preferences → General → Check for updates. The Microsoft Store version is updated by the Store and does not perform this check.

Notices and configuration data

From CopyC 1.1.1, every version (including the Microsoft Store version) downloads a data file signed by us from our website (copyc.app.dreamsworld.top) when it starts and every 6 hours after that. It only contains notices, names of password managers to mask, corrections to interface text, and switches to pause sync features if a service has a problem; it contains no code, and a file with a wrong signature is never used. The request contains nothing you copied, no account details and no personal data; as with any web request, Cloudflare sees your IP address.

What CopyC never does

  • Without sync, it never sends what you copy over the network; with sync, it only sends encrypted text content automatically.
  • It never sends your files or photos on its own: only files you choose with Send files are sent, encrypted, to your own devices.
  • It contains no analytics, ads or trackers.
  • It needs no CopyC account; sync uses your own Google account and is optional.
  • It never sells or shares data with third parties.

This website

This website (copyc.app.dreamsworld.top) is hosted on Cloudflare and uses cookie-free Cloudflare Web Analytics to count anonymous visits. The download section asks GitHub for the latest version number. The live sync relay also runs on this domain, as described under “Sync across devices” above.

Children's privacy

CopyC does not collect personal data from anyone on our side, including children under 13. Sync requires a Google account and is subject to Google's age requirements for accounts.

Future features

CopyC keeps growing. If a future version adds any new feature that lets data leave this computer (for example sending files over the internet), we will update this policy before it ships, explaining what is sent, where it goes, how long it is kept and how to turn it off. You will decide whether to turn such features on.
