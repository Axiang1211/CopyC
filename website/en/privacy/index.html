Privacy Policy

Your clipboard goes only where you decide.

By default CopyC works entirely on your computer. Only if you sign in with Google to turn on sync is text content encrypted on your computer first and then sent to your other computers and your own Google Drive. We never sell or share it, and we can't read what you copy.

Policy version 1.2 · Last updated: October 27, 2026 · Applies to all versions of CopyC (Windows, macOS and Microsoft Store versions)

  • Local by default Without sync, what you copy is only used for the confirmation and your history on your own computer. It never leaves it.
  • Sync is optional and encrypted Sync is off by default. When on, content is encrypted on your computer, so the relay server can't read it. Images, files and passwords are never synced.
  • No tracking, no selling No analytics, ads or trackers, and nothing is ever sold or shared.

What CopyC reads

  • Clipboard content: each time you copy, CopyC reads the clipboard to work out what kind of content it is (text, image, files, color, link) and show a confirmation. It listens to the system clipboard event and does not log keystrokes.
  • The foreground app's name: to show which app you copied from and to apply your app blocklist.
  • Whether an app is fullscreen: to switch to an unobtrusive indicator while you watch videos, play games or present.

What CopyC stores on your computer

  • History: the text, links and color codes you copy, plus image thumbnails and file locations (paths), are saved in a local database on your computer so you can search and copy them again later. By default up to 1,000 entries are kept and the oldest are removed automatically. You can change the limit (100–5,000), delete single entries, clear everything, or turn history off in Preferences → History. Files themselves are never copied or stored.
  • Passwords are not saved by default: content that password managers mark as confidential, and content that looks like a password or key, is not written to history by default. It is only saved if you turn on “Save passwords and sensitive content” (stored in plain text on your computer and still masked in the list). Turning that option off deletes the saved passwords.
  • App blocklist: nothing copied from an app on your blocklist is written to history or synced.
  • “Recent copies” on the Overview: up to 20 items, kept in memory only and gone when you quit the app.
  • Your settings: such as position, language and app blocklist, saved in a settings file on your computer.
  • If you sign in to sync: your Google account email, your Google account ID, a randomly generated device ID, and the authorization token (refresh token) Google issues to CopyC. On Windows the token is kept in Credential Manager; on macOS in a settings file only you can read.

To remove everything, click Clear in Preferences → History (and, if you use sync, Delete cloud data in Preferences → Sync), then uninstall. The Microsoft Store version deletes all of its local data when uninstalled; for the other versions the data lives in “%APPDATA%\top.dreamsworld.copyc” on Windows and “~/Library/Application Support/top.dreamsworld.copyc” on macOS, and you can delete it by hand.

Sync across devices (optional, from 1.1.0)

Sync is off by default and only starts when you click “Sign in with Google” in Preferences → Sync or in the welcome guide. Once on:

  • What is synced: text content only — text, links, email addresses, phone numbers and color codes — together with when it was copied and this computer's name.
  • Never synced: images, files and file paths, passwords and sensitive content (whether or not you save passwords), and anything copied from a blocklisted app. Single entries larger than 256 KB are not synced either.
  • End-to-end encryption: content is encrypted on your computer with XChaCha20-Poly1305. The encryption key is created on the first device you sign in on and stored in CopyC's private folder in your Google Drive, so your other computers on the same Google account can decrypt it. Neither Cloudflare nor our server ever has the key or can read the content.
  • History backup (Google Drive): encrypted entries are stored in the application data folder (appDataFolder) of your own Google Drive. This folder is hidden from your files, and CopyC can only access this folder — never your other files. Backups are kept for 90 days and then deleted by CopyC. Entries you delete on one computer are deleted on the others too.
  • Live sync (Cloudflare): when you copy text, the encrypted content passes through our relay server on Cloudflare (copyc.app.dreamsworld.top) to your other computers that are signed in to the same Google account and online, which put it on their clipboard (you can turn this off). The relay only uses your Google sign-in token to tell which account you belong to; it stores no content and does not store your email. As with any network connection, Cloudflare processes connection details such as your IP address.
  • Google permissions: CopyC only asks for openid, email (to show which account you signed in with) and drive.appdata (read and write CopyC's own hidden folder only).

You can turn off live sync or history backup at any time. Sign out revokes CopyC's access, deletes the token on this computer and removes entries synced from your other computers; Delete cloud data removes all of CopyC's backups and keys from your Google Drive. You can also remove CopyC under third-party access in your Google Account.

CopyC's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements: this data is only used to provide the sync features you can see, is never used for advertising, never sold and never given to third parties, and is not read by humans.

Masking and Privacy mode

Content that password managers mark as confidential, and content that looks like a password or key, is masked in confirmations automatically. With Privacy mode on, confirmations, recent copies and the History list show only the content type, never the content, which is handy when sharing your screen.

Update check

The version downloaded from the website asks GitHub for the latest version number when it starts (and once a day while it keeps running), and lets you know if a new version is available on the website. The request contains nothing you copied and no personal data; as with any web request, GitHub sees your IP address. You can turn it off in Preferences → General → Check for updates. The Microsoft Store version is updated by the Store and does not perform this check.

What CopyC never does

  • Without sync, it never sends what you copy over the network; with sync, it only sends encrypted text content.
  • It contains no analytics, ads or trackers.
  • It needs no CopyC account; sync uses your own Google account and is optional.
  • It never sells or shares data with third parties.

This website

This website (copyc.app.dreamsworld.top) is hosted on Cloudflare and uses cookie-free Cloudflare Web Analytics to count anonymous visits. The download section asks GitHub for the latest version number. The live sync relay also runs on this domain, as described under “Sync across devices” above.

Children's privacy

CopyC does not collect personal data from anyone on our side, including children under 13. Sync requires a Google account and is subject to Google's age requirements for accounts.

Future features

CopyC keeps growing. If a future version adds any new feature that lets data leave this computer (for example sending files directly over your local network), we will update this policy before it ships, explaining what is sent, where it goes, how long it is kept and how to turn it off. You will decide whether to turn such features on.
